## Flight Pattern Analysis

### Fixed-Wing (Pilatus PC-12)

**Typical Mission Profile**:
- **Cruise Altitude**: ~7,000 feet (higher than helicopters)
- **Cruise Speed**: ~200 knots (much faster than helicopters)
- **Range**: Suitable for inter-hospital transfers across multiple states
- **Mission Type**: Long-distance medical transport, organ transport, critical patient transfers

**Flight Pattern**: Point-to-point flights between hospitals, typically at higher altitudes for fuel efficiency and speed.

### Rotary-Wing (AgustaWestland AW119)

**Typical Mission Profile**:
- **Operating Altitude**: Variable, often low-level for direct hospital-to-hospital
- **Speed**: ~100-130 knots
- **Range**: Regional operations within Minnesota and neighboring states
- **Mission Type**: Emergency scene response, trauma transfers, quick hospital-to-hospital

**Flight Pattern**: More flexible routing, can land at hospitals without airport facilities, often seen at lower altitudes.

## Comparison: Fixed-Wing vs. Rotary-Wing

| Factor | Fixed-Wing (PC-12) | Rotary-Wing (AW119) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Speed** | 200+ knots | 100-130 knots |
| **Range** | 1,800+ nm | 350 nm |
| **Landing Sites** | Airports only | Hospitals, scenes, parking lots |
| **Altitude** | 10,000-25,000 ft typical | Low-level to 10,000 ft |
| **Weather Capability** | Better (pressurized, de-icing) | Limited in icing/low visibility |
| **Patient Capacity** | More spacious, 2+ patients | 1-2 patients (cramped) |
| **Operating Cost** | Higher fuel, lower hourly | Lower fuel, higher hourly |
| **Best For** | Long distance, stable patients | Trauma, short distance, scene calls |
